Range Summary window

1 of 4

The problem

Analysts pick date ranges on a trend chart to compare periods, like this fall against last winter. Each range opened its own floating window. Comparing three periods meant juggling three windows, reading numbers across them by eye, and doing the subtraction in your head. Windows could also stay open after their chart was gone.

What I made

  • All ranges live in one window, with a Swap view option to flip rows and columns.
  • Selected ranges are shaded on the chart and update the moment one is added or removed.
  • Show differences adds later-minus-earlier comparisons. Any pair can be chosen, swapped or removed, shown as values, percent change, or both, grouped at the end or beside each range.
  • Differences read the way the source numbers do: same units, an up or down arrow, and percentage rows in points rather than a percent of a percent.
  • When two ranges have different lengths, Totals differences are greyed out because they mostly reflect the length. Averages stay normal.
  • Headers show readable dates, the comparison period and the range length, like "Dec 28, 1998 – Feb 21, 1999, 8 weeks".
  • Everything works from the keyboard, with screen-reader labels built from the dates and no switch animation when reduced motion is on.
  • The window closes with its chart every time, so no more orphaned windows.

Elmira College, web design

College website redesign

1 of 3

The problem

Prospective students and their families came to the site with specific questions: how to visit, how to apply, what it costs. The answers were there, but they took too long to find, and the things that set the college apart were easy to miss.

What I made

  • A new design built around ease of use, so visitors find what they came for in fewer clicks.
  • Menus grouped by what people are trying to do, like Visit, Apply, and Tuition and Financial Aid, instead of by how the college is organized.
  • Calls to action like Apply and Visit pinned to the top of every page.
  • Important parts of the college, like news, events, open houses and the virtual tour, given room on the home page.
  • A consistent page layout with breadcrumbs and section menus, so visitors always know where they are.

Directory redesign

1 of 3

The problem

Finding a dashboard meant knowing who saved it and which folder they used. Bookmarks and dashboards were mixed together, and there was no good way to search.

Try the interactive mockup (opens in a new tab)

What I made

  • Search first, across names, tags and descriptions.
  • Filters to show everything, only dashboards or only bookmarks.
  • Recent, Favorites and Featured at the top of the folder tree.
  • Grid and list views, and three densities from compact to detailed.
  • A clickable mockup used to gather feedback from the team.
